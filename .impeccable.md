## Design Context

This context applies to atlcli's generated documents and to the browser-extension
Publishing Studio. Each surface has its own aesthetic constraints below.

### Users

atlcli serves technical and business users who work with Atlassian content and need dependable exports for review, sharing, archiving, and further editing. Exported documents must remain comfortable to read across long, dense Confluence pages with prose, lists, tables, diagrams, and screenshots.

### Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and trustworthy. atlcli should feel fast and technically rigorous without looking developer-only, decorative, or tied to a specific customer brand.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined editorial-document direction: neutral light surfaces, restrained blue accents, strong information hierarchy, generous but efficient vertical rhythm, and print-quality typography. The built-in PDF design must be brand-neutral and reproducible with bundled assets. Preserve the running title, fine horizontal rule, and centered page number as the export's recognizable navigation system. Do not transfer this page-layout aesthetic to the extension UI without its separate design context.

### Design Principles

1. Optimize first for sustained reading and scanning in long documents.
2. Use spacing to express semantic hierarchy; keep related content grouped and sections clearly separated.
3. Preserve author intent when explicit layout information exists, and apply conservative content-aware defaults otherwise.
4. Prefer deterministic, accessible, locally bundled output over decorative or environment-dependent styling.
5. Make performance and degradation visible so users can trust the export result.

### Extension Publishing Studio

The extension serves Confluence authors and publishing operators who need to configure,
preview, validate, and export documents without leaving the page they are working on.
They may be non-technical, but they care about reliable output and need to understand
degradation without reading engine terminology.

The Studio should feel **compact, precise, and calm**. Use an Atlassian-adjacent shell for
familiar navigation, editorially precise configuration hierarchy, and a creator-studio
preview that makes the document the focal point. The dominant mode is a narrow browser
sidebar, so density and progressive disclosure matter more than generous marketing-page
spacing. Keep light neutral surfaces with restrained teal accents; do not imitate a
specific customer theme.

Design the primary path as scope → format → design → review/export. Keep advanced controls
collapsed, reserve cards for actionable or stateful units, and avoid nested-card chrome.
Small visual controls still need accessible names, visible keyboard focus, and practical
pointer targets. Use color together with labels and icons, never as the only status signal.
During warnings and failures, prioritize a plain-language summary, affected-content count,
and next action; keep technical detail available through progressive disclosure.
